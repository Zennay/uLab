# Code of Conduct

uLab aims to be a technically rigorous, respectful open-source project. Participation should make it easier to review evidence, challenge assumptions, and improve the software without making contributors feel personally targeted.

## Expected behavior

When participating in the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, or other project spaces:

- discuss the work, evidence, and trade-offs rather than attacking people;
- give specific, actionable feedback and be willing to explain technical claims;
- assume good faith while still asking for reproducible proof;
- respect different backgrounds, experience levels, identities, and communication styles;
- keep disagreements focused on the project;
- respect privacy and do not publish someone else's private information.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable:

- harassment, intimidation, threats, stalking, or sustained personal attacks;
- discriminatory or degrading language or behavior;
- unwanted sexual attention or sexualized content directed at another participant;
- doxxing or sharing private information without permission;
- deliberate disruption of project collaboration after a maintainer asks it to stop;
- retaliation against someone for raising a conduct concern in good faith.

Technical disagreement, direct review comments, rejecting a proposal, or asking for stronger evidence are not conduct violations by themselves.

## Scope

This applies in uLab-managed project spaces and when someone is clearly representing the project in another community.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not post sensitive conduct reports publicly.

If GitHub provides a private reporting or moderation channel for the repository, use that. Otherwise, open a minimal public issue asking for a private maintainer contact method without including names, screenshots, private messages, or other sensitive details.

Maintainers may remove content, issue a warning, restrict participation, or block a participant when necessary to protect the project community. Decisions should be proportionate to the behavior and documented privately where public detail would expose sensitive information.

A report made in good faith will not be treated as misconduct simply because the maintainers ultimately reach a different conclusion.
